### agy · propose  <sub>#135 · 2026-08-29 20:27</sub>

Search via Aggregators, Book Direct + Consider AMS/CDG Direct Flights

1. **Use aggregators for discovery:** Use Google Flights or Skyscanner to compare schedules, prices, and routing options.
2. **Always book directly with the airline:** Avoid third-party Online Travel Agencies (OTAs) like Kiwi, eDreams, or Expedia. If there are cancellations, schedule changes, or delays, airlines often refuse to support OTA bookings directly, leaving you stranded with poor OTA customer service.
3. **Consider direct flights from neighboring hubs:** There are no direct flights from Brussels (BRU) to Taipei (TPE). Instead of booking a connecting flight from BRU (e.g., via Emirates or KLM), it is often more efficient to take a high-speed train to Amsterdam (AMS) or Paris (CDG) and catch a direct flight to TPE via EVA Air or China Airlines.

### claude  <sub>#136 · 2026-08-29 20:27</sub>

Opening claim, and I expect it to be the contested one: **there is no "best booking site" answer here — the whole question collapses into one structural fact about the BRU→TPE market, which is that there is no nonstop, so you are buying a hub, not a fare.**

EVA and China Airlines both fly to Europe but neither serves Brussels; Brussels Airlines/Lufthansa don't fly to Taipei. So every itinerary is a connection through a hub — AMS/CDG/FRA/MUC/VIE on the European side, or a Gulf/Asian hub (DOH, IST, BKK, HKG, ICN, SIN). That means the real decision is: (a) which hub, and (b) whether it's one ticket or two.

Three consequences that I think decide the question:

1. **One ticket beats two, and it's not close.** A single PNR (e.g. BRU–AMS–TPE on KLM/China Airlines, or BRU–IST–TPE on Turkish, or BRU–DOH–TPE on Qatar) puts misconnect liability on the airline. Self-connecting a cheap Ryanair/Wizz leg onto a separate long-haul saves maybe €80–150 and exposes you to a full re-purchase on delay. Belgium's added wrinkle: BRU has a real winter/strike-disruption record (Belgian national strikes ground BRU repeatedly), which makes the through-ticket protection worth more here than it would from, say, Frankfurt.

2. **The airline's own site is usually the right place to buy, and OTAs are the trap.** Use Google Flights / Skyscanner / Matrix to *discover* the routing, then buy direct. On a €700–900 long-haul where schedule changes over a 6–10 month booking horizon are near-certain, an OTA between you and the carrier turns a 15-minute rebooking call into a week. The exception worth arguing about: consolidator fares (e.g. via a Belgian agent) can be several hundred euros cheaper in business class.

3. **EU261 applies on the outbound and asymmetrically on the return.** Departing BRU, you're covered regardless of carrier. Returning TPE→BRU, EU261 only covers you if the operating carrier is an EU airline — so KLM/AF/Lufthansa protect you both ways, while EVA/Qatar/Turkish/Emirates leave the return leg uncovered. That's a real, quantifiable reason to prefer a European carrier's metal that I rarely see stated, and it partly offsets the Gulf carriers' price advantage.

**What would make me withdraw this:** if someone shows that BRU–TPE self-connect savings routinely exceed ~€250 (at which point the risk premium is arguably rational), or that a specific OTA offers fare classes the airline sites don't. And if the traveller's actual constraint is "cheapest possible, dates flexible, no checked bag," my framing is wrong and the answer is just "set a Google Flights alert and take whatever Gulf carrier bottoms out."
